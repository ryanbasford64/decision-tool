Note on the Bronze numbers. These figures assume the Reserve commission route — commission as a 2nd Lt and immediately apply to SJPD, drilling one weekend a month as a commissioned officer. Officer drill pay is higher than enlisted drill, which is why Bronze edges ahead of Green/Blue by 28. Alternative · Active duty commission

If you commission active duty instead of Reserve, the civilian career delays four years — but you separate as a Captain at 28 with a security clearance and federal LE eligibility. Lower pay through 28 (~$80-141K total), much higher ceiling after.

The honest tradeoff

If you commission into the Reserves, the Bronze Path arrives at the same civilian destination as Green and Blue on the same timeline — same SJPD academy at 22, same top-step paycheck at 28 — but with officer rank, higher drill pay, and federal/corporate doors still open.

The trade is the four years of college plus ROTC instead of enlisting at 18. You give up early enlisted experience and the specific tradecraft of a 12B or Seabee. In exchange you get a degree before starting the civilian career, an officer commission, and the kind of optionality at 30 that very few people your age will have.

If you commission active duty instead, the civilian career delays four years. The ceiling after separation is materially higher (federal LE, defense contracting at $150-250K), but the SJPD-by-22 timeline is gone.

Which version is right depends on whether you want the civilian badge as fast as possible, or want to lead soldiers as a commissioned officer while building it.

03 · Before You Sign

Six questions worth asking.

Ask these of every recruiter, and of yourself. If a recruiter pressures you to sign before you've answered them, that's the answer.

Why am I drawn to enlist now rather than commission at 22?

Is it the identity? The structure? Friends doing it? A specific recruiter? Be honest — the answer tells you which path fits.

What does the career I want at 30 actually prefer?

Whatever you're aiming at — police, federal LE, corporate, something else — call somebody doing it now. Ask: "What does your strongest applicant look like at 25?" The answer is free and specific.

Am I optimizing for paychecks through 28, or for options after 28?

That's the central trade in this document. Different answers point to different paths.

What happens if my Reserve unit activates during junior year?

Ask the recruiter. Ask SJSU's veteran services office. Plan for it — don't hope it doesn't happen.

Which military job leaves me with the broadest set of civilian options?

Combat Engineer teaches tactics and demolitions. Seabee teaches construction and small-unit ops. An officer commission teaches leadership at scale. They're different, not equivalent — and they open different doors.

Have I actually visited a Navy recruiter?

If not, that's a gap. Closing it costs you one afternoon.

The decision is yours

Nobody should make this choice for you. But you owe it to the person you'll be at 28 to make it deliberately, after talking to all three branches and to someone working in the career you think you want.
